Souffleur

Your lines, right under the camera.

Read aloud: the words you say dim, the next one lights up, and the line you are on stays right under the lens.

Souffleur is a free, open source teleprompter for the Mac. It lives in the notch, right beside your camera, and follows your voice word by word, so you keep eye contact on every call, video and talk.

Why Souffleur

The theatre’s oldest trick, in your notch.

In the theatre, the souffleur hides in a small box at the edge of the stage and whispers the next line to the actor. On a MacBook, that box is already there: the notch, a few millimetres from the lens.

Look at the camera, not at a script in the corner of your screen. Souffleur puts your words where your eyes should be.

Voice follow

It listens. It keeps your place.

Souffleur hears your words on your Mac and lines them up with your script. The words you have said dim, the next one lights up, and the line you are on stays under the camera. Stop to breathe and it waits. Skip a sentence and it finds you again.

Say it

The next word lights up

Numbers, contractions and accents are understood, so “25” meets “twenty-five”.

Pause

It waits for you

Nothing rolls while you think, drink or improvise. Your place is kept.

Skip

It finds you again

Jump a paragraph and a few words are enough to pick you up further down.

Voice Follow Default

Word by word, from what you say. For scripts you mean to read as written.

Voice Pace

Rolls at your pace while you speak, waits when you stop. Any language.

Auto Scroll

A steady pace in words per minute, faster or slower as you go.

Manual

Only your keys, your clicker, your phone or your trackpad move it.

Three places

In the notch, on your screen, or on a rig.

The notch keeps your eyes a few millimetres from the lens. Without a notch, or with a camera on top of an external display, the prompter floats wherever you want it. For a teleprompter mirror, it fills any display, flipped.

Notch

Compact, three lines under the camera, with the time on one side of the lens and your voice on the other.

Floating

A card of glass you drag anywhere, under a webcam or beside your slides. It remembers where you left it.

Full screen

Any display, a Sidecar iPad or a beam-splitter rig, mirrored horizontally, vertically or both.

Control

Hands free. Eyes up.

Drive a take without looking away: shortcuts that work in every app, the presentation clicker or foot pedal you already own, or your phone.

Shortcuts, everywhere

They work while you are in Zoom, Keynote or your editor, and need no permission.

  • Prompt, play or pause ⌃⌥⌘P
  • Faster, slower ⌃⌥⌘↑↓
  • Back or forward a line ⌃⌥⌘←→
  • Restart ⌃⌥⌘R
  • Show or hide ⌃⌥⌘H
  • Presentation clicker, foot pedal Page UpPage Down

Clicker keys only belong to Souffleur while the prompter is open. Links such as souffleur://prompt?text=Hello and Shortcuts actions run it from anywhere else.

Your phone is the remote

Turn it on in Settings and scan the code: a page opens on your phone with play, pause, pace and the line you are on. Nothing to install.

Only your network, only the phone that scanned the code. Off until you turn it on.

Privacy

It hears you. It tells no one.

Your voice stays on your Mac

Speech is recognised on the Mac, never sent to a server. If your Mac cannot recognise a language offline, Souffleur rolls at your pace instead.

No account, no tracking

No sign in and no analytics in the app. It connects only to check for updates, and to your phone when you turn the remote on.

Honest about screen sharing

The prompter is kept out of captures that respect macOS’s protection. Since macOS 15 some apps capture it anyway, so share a window, not your whole screen.

Open source

Every line is on GitHub.

Souffleur is MIT licensed: read exactly what runs on your Mac, change it, ship your own. Issues and pull requests are welcome.

Questions

Does my Mac need a notch?

No. On a Mac without a notch, the prompter hangs from the middle of the menu bar, or floats wherever you put it, or fills an external display.

Does it send my voice anywhere?

No. Recognition runs on your Mac. If your Mac cannot recognise the script’s language offline, Souffleur switches to Voice Pace, which only listens for when you are speaking.

Which languages does voice follow understand?

Those your Mac recognises on device, which covers English, French, German, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese, Chinese, Japanese and more. Souffleur listens in the language your script is written in, or the one you choose. Voice Pace and Auto Scroll work in any language.

Will people see it when I share my screen?

Apps that respect macOS’s capture protection will not. Since macOS 15, apps that record the screen with ScreenCaptureKit can capture it anyway, whatever any teleprompter says. Share a window rather than your whole screen, and nobody sees your script.

Can I use it with a teleprompter mirror?

Yes. Choose Full Screen, pick the display under the glass, and mirror the text horizontally, vertically or both.

Does it work with my presentation clicker?

Most clickers and foot pedals send Page Up and Page Down. While the prompter is open, those move the script, and Page Down starts a paused take.

Can I control it from my phone?

Yes. Turn on the phone remote in Settings, Controls, and scan the code. Your phone and your Mac need to be on the same network; only a phone that scanned the code can connect.

How do I remove it?

Quit Souffleur and move it to the Trash. Your scripts are in ~/Library/Application Support/Souffleur; with Homebrew, brew uninstall --cask --zap souffleur.

Is Souffleur made by Apple?

No. Souffleur is an independent open source project and is not affiliated with Apple.
